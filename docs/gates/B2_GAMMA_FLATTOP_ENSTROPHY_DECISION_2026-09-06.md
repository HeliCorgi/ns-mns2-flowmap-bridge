## 1. Exact physical vorticity carried by a Gamma transition

For axisymmetric swirl,

\[
 u^\theta=\frac{\Gamma}{r}.
\]

The poloidal vorticity components generated by the swirl are exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \omega^r=-\partial_z u^\theta=-\frac{\partial_z\Gamma}{r},
 \qquad
 \omega^z=\frac1r\partial_r(r u^\theta)=\frac{\partial_r\Gamma}{r}.
 }
 \tag{1.1}
\]

Therefore their physical `L^2(R^3)` contribution is

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal E_\Gamma(t)
 :=\int_{\mathbb R^3}\bigl((\omega^r)^2+(\omega^z)^2\bigr)\,dx
 =2\pi\int_{\mathbb R}\int_0^\infty
 \frac{|\partial_r\Gamma|^2+|\partial_z\Gamma|^2}{r}\,dr\,dz.
 }
 \tag{1.2}
\]

For smooth divergence-free fields,

\[
 \|\nabla u\|_2^2=\|\omega\|_2^2\ge \mathcal E_\Gamma,
\]

so the standard energy inequality gives the genuine finite budget

\[
 \boxed{
 \nu\int_0^{T_*}\mathcal E_\Gamma(t)\,dt
 \le \frac12\|u_0\|_2^2.
 }
 \tag{1.3}
\]

This is the strongest immediately available global finite budget to charge the transition against.

## 2. A linewise transition inequality

Fix one time and one axial coordinate `z`. Smooth axisymmetry gives

\[
 \Gamma(0,z)=0.
\]

If for some `R>0`

\[
 |\Gamma(R,z)|\ge m>0,
 \tag{2.1}
\]

then Cauchy--Schwarz with weights `r^{-1}` and `r` gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 m
 &\le \int_0^R |\partial_r\Gamma(r,z)|\,dr\\
 &\le
 \left(\int_0^R\frac{|\partial_r\Gamma|^2}{r}\,dr\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\int_0^R r\,dr\right)^{1/2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^R\frac{|\partial_r\Gamma(r,z)|^2}{r}\,dr
 \ge \frac{2m^2}{R^2}.
 }
 \tag{2.2}
\]

If a fixed fraction of the non-evanescent circulation is reached at radius at most `R` for every
`z` in a measurable set `Z` of length `L_z`, then

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal E_\Gamma(t)
 \ge 4\pi\,\frac{m^2 L_z}{R^2}.
 }
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Thus a genuinely thick saturation sheet does have a compulsory physical enstrophy cost.

## 3. The compulsory cost is still time-integrable in the frozen middle limb

Take `R~tau^{beta_v}` and `m~Gamma0`.

### 3.1 Saturation region with its own isotropic scale

If the high-`Gamma` region has axial thickness comparable to its radial scale,

\[
 L_z\asymp R\asymp\tau^{\beta_v},
\]

then (2.3) gives only

\[
 \mathcal E_\Gamma(t)\gtrsim \tau^{-\beta_v}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

But every middle-limb exponent satisfies

\[
 \beta_v<\gamma<1,
\]

so

\[
 \int_0 \tau^{-\beta_v}\,d\tau<\infty.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the physical energy-dissipation budget does **not** kill an `R`-sized flat-top/saturation blob.
This is exactly the same scaling already visible in the Stage-9 `S` region: a Gamma-saturated blob
with velocity amplitude `~R^{-1}` and volume `~R^3` has enstrophy `~R^{-1}`.

Then

\[
 \mathcal E_\Gamma(t)\gtrsim \tau^{\alpha-2\beta_v}.

So even this stronger sheet cost is automatically integrable throughout the frozen middle limb.
The proposed enstrophy cut is already dominated by the existing K5/dissipation wedge.

## 4. The transition nevertheless forces large pointwise vorticity

At a fixed `z` satisfying (2.1), the mean-value theorem gives some `r_* in (0,R)` with

\[
 |\partial_r\Gamma(r_*,z)|\ge \frac{m}{R}.
\]

Using `r_*<=R` in (1.1),

\[
 \boxed{
 |\omega^z(r_*,z)|
 =\frac{|\partial_r\Gamma(r_*,z)|}{r_*}
 \ge \frac{m}{R^2}.
 }
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Therefore any non-evanescent saturation at `R~tau^{beta_v}` forces

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\omega(t)\|_\infty\gtrsim \tau^{-2\beta_v}.
 }
 \tag{4.2}
\]

For `beta_v>=1/2`, this lower bound is non-integrable in time. That is not a contradiction for a
hypothetical breakdown trajectory; it is the correct direction for a Beale--Kato--Majda-type
necessary divergence. The transition layer therefore trades the forbidden curvature at the
Gamma maximum for large **off-maximum swirl-gradient vorticity**.

### Decision

 \boxed{\texttt{B2-GAMMA-TRANSITION-VORTICITY = YES}.}
 \tag{4.3}
\]

This is an actual geometric consequence of `Gamma(0,z)=0` plus non-evanescent saturation.

## 5. Pointwise flatness does not imply a thick high-Gamma neighborhood

The previous curvature gate controls only the Hessian trace at the active maximum. Even if the
maximum becomes exactly flat at second order, uncontrolled higher derivatives can place the
transition arbitrarily close to the flat top. There is no theorem in the frozen stack converting
small maximum curvature into a lower bound for the measure of a high-`Gamma` superlevel set.

The obstruction can be made explicit by a meridional logarithmic-capacity profile.

Fix `R>0`, choose `0<a<L<R/4`, and let

\[
 \rho=\sqrt{(r-R)^2+z^2}.
\]

Start from the piecewise logarithmic profile

\[
 G_{a,L}(\rho)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&0\le\rho\le a,\\
 \dfrac{\log(L/\rho)}{\log(L/a)},&a<\rho<L,\\
 0,&\rho\ge L,
 \end{cases}
 \tag{5.1}
\]

and smooth the two corners without changing the estimates by more than fixed factors. Define

\[
 \Gamma_{R,a,L}(r,z)=M G_{a,L}(\rho).
 \tag{5.2}
\]

Because the support stays away from the axis, `u^theta=Gamma/r` is a smooth compactly supported
axisymmetric swirl field; taking the velocity to be pure swirl gives a real smooth divergence-free
`C_c^infinity(R^3)` datum. The center is an exact flat plateau: all spatial derivatives vanish
there before the transition begins.

On the support, `r~R`. Direct integration gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \int\!\!\int \frac{|\nabla\Gamma_{R,a,L}|^2}{r}\,dr\,dz
 \lesssim
 \frac{M^2}{R\log(L/a)}.
 }
 \tag{5.3}
\]

Indeed `|G'|=1/(rho log(L/a))` on the logarithmic annulus, so the unweighted two-dimensional
Dirichlet cost is `~1/log(L/a)`, and `1/r~1/R` there.

For every fixed fraction `c' in (0,1)`, the `c'` level approaches the center radius `R` as
`a/L->0`; hence the first fixed-fraction saturation radius remains comparable to `R` while the
weighted enstrophy cost can be reduced by the arbitrarily large logarithm `log(L/a)`.

This proves a precise warning:

> a single saturation point plus second-order flatness at the maximum does **not** force a
> quantitative `L^2` transition cost. A thickness / capacity hypothesis is additional structure,
> not a consequence of the current curvature theorem.

These profiles are only instantaneous admissible data snapshots, not B2 singular trajectories. Their
role is to falsify any attempted universal instantaneous inequality that would deduce a strong
enstrophy lower bound solely from `r_sat` and maximum flatness.

## 6. Why vorticity production does not close the kill with known budgets

The full vorticity enstrophy identity is

\[
 \frac12\frac d{dt}\|\omega\|_2^2
 +\nu\|\nabla\omega\|_2^2
 =\int_{\mathbb R^3}(\omega\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\omega\,dx.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

A steep transition may make both palinstrophy and the stretching term very large. In three
dimensions, however, neither side has a sign that supplies an independent finite budget up to a
hypothetical singular time. The energy inequality controls the **time integral of enstrophy**,
not the time integral of palinstrophy or the absolute vorticity-production term.

Therefore charging the flat-top transition to (6.1) would require a new estimate controlling the
stretching source strongly enough to dominate the transition. No such estimate is present in the
frozen B2 stack, and assuming a Serrin/BKM-equivalent control would simply import a regularity
criterion.

So this pass does **not** prove that every possible vorticity-production argument fails. It proves
that the standard physical production identity, with the budgets presently available in the
project, does not kill the flat-top branch.

## 7. Structural interpretation

The curvature gate and the present enstrophy gate together force a useful relocation of the
mechanism.

For `beta_v>=1/2` a one-scale curvature peak at the non-evanescent Gamma maximum is impossible.
A surviving flat-top realization can instead have

1. small/zero curvature at the maximum;
2. a transition layer away from the maximum where `|omega^z|` is at least `~R^{-2}`;
3. an `L^2` enstrophy cost that is nevertheless time-integrable because the high-vorticity region
   can have sufficiently small meridional measure.

Thus the next obstruction, if one exists, must control **where the transition layer goes and how long
high-Gamma material can reside near the axis**, not merely its instantaneous `L^2` cost.

This is stronger information than the old exponent witness but still does not kill the middle limb.
