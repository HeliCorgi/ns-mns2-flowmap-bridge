## §1 — Conventions (matched to the GPT-side files) and the analytic identification [D]

`A = ∇u` (`A_{ij} = ∂_iu_j`), `S = \mathrm{sym}A`, `q = ωᵀSω`, `\det S = λ₁λ₂λ₃`. The GPT-side files define `β_B(Ω) := (−4∫_Ω\det S)/(∫_Ω q)` (determinant source over stretching); the top-θ sets are `Ω_θ = \{q > \mathrm{quantile}_{1−θ}(q \mid q>0)\}` (the quantile is taken among positive-`q` points — their volume fractions `≈ θ·\mathrm{frac}(q>0)` fix this), the intense mask is `\{λ₂>0\}∩\{|ω|>0.25Λ\}`. With the local Betchov identity `q + 4\det S = \mathrm{div}J_B`, `J_B = \tfrac43[(u·∇u)·∇u − ½u\,\mathrm{tr}A²]`, and the enstrophy equation `∂_t\tfrac{|ω|²}{2} + \mathrm{div}(u\tfrac{|ω|²}{2} − ν∇\tfrac{|ω|²}{2} − J_B) = −4\det S − ν|∇ω|²`, one has `∫_Ω q = −4∫_Ω\det S + ∮_{∂Ω}J_B·n`, so **`β_B(Ω) < 1 ⟺ ∮_{∂Ω}J_B·n > 0 ⟺ the modified flux `−J_B` converges into `Ω`** ("flux-fed"); `β_B(𝕋³) = 1` exactly.

**Identification [D].** For traceless `A`, Cayley–Hamilton gives `\mathrm{tr}A³ = 3\det A`, and `\mathrm{tr}A³ = \mathrm{tr}S³ + 3\mathrm{tr}(SΩ²) = 3\det S + ¾q`. Hence

> **`q + 4\det S = \tfrac43\mathrm{tr}A³ = 4\det(∇u)`, i.e. `1 − β_B(Ω) = 4∫_Ω\det(∇u)\big/∫_Ω q`.**

The "Betchov-flux fraction" of a region is the regional integral of the **third invariant of the velocity-gradient tensor** (`R = −\det A` in the classical `(Q,R)` notation) normalised by the stretching; `\det(∇u)` is an algebraic pointwise quantity, and its being a divergence (`4\det A = \mathrm{div}J_B`) is the reason its global integral vanishes. The flux language is therefore *bookkeeping* for the sign-indefinite invariant `\det(∇u)`, not a transport of enstrophy: "flux-fed hot core" means "the hot core sits at `\det(∇u) > 0`" (the `R < 0` side), "source-dominated" means `\det(∇u) ≈ 0` there. Pointwise, on a core with `ω ∥ e_k`, `q ≈ λ_k|ω|²` and `β_{loc} ≈ −4\prod_{i≠k}λ_i/|ω|²`: for `ω ∥ e₃` (extensional alignment) `β_{loc} ≈ 4|λ₁|λ₂/|ω|²` — small when `λ₂` is small, *negative* for uniaxial extension (`λ₂ < 0`, Burgers-type stretching, `\det S > 0`); for `ω ∥ e₂` (the DNS-generic alignment) `β_{loc} ≈ 4|λ₁|λ₃/|ω|² = O(1)`. **So `β_B` on cores is a strain-geometry / alignment ratio (which eigen-direction carries `ω`, and the strain-to-enstrophy ratio in the normal plane), and the E0-versus-E3 contrast is predicted by the alignment already on file (`cos²θ₂ ≈ 0.3` in E0/E1, `≈ 0.8` in E3, twenty-seventh record §4).** Whether `β_B` is nevertheless an *independent* coordinate from C1 and C4 in the data is the empirical question of §4.

## §5 — Are `(C1, β_B(θ), C4)` independent mechanism axes?

Within a single event every quantity is a function of time along one trajectory, so the within-event correlations are *trajectory* correlations, and their signs flip across runs (C1 vs β(top1): `−0.99, −0.83, +0.90, +0.67, −0.03`) — they cannot establish independence. Across runs (five points): **C1 and C4 co-vary** (both fall from the TG data to the shear/random/tube data: C1 `0.28, 0.30, 0.18, 0.20, 0.08`; C4 `0.49, 0.29, 0.06, 0.10, −0.04`; rank correlation `≈ +0.9`) — they are essentially one *datum/intensity* axis; **β(top1) does not follow it** (`0.07, 0.15, 0.79, 0.63, 0.52`; the tube run has the lowest C1 and C4 but a middle β). So β is a second coordinate — **but not a new one**: by §1, `1 − β_B(Ω) = 4∫_Ω\det(∇u)/∫_Ω q` and on cores `β_{loc} ≈ −4\prod_{i≠k}λ_i/|ω|²` for `ω ∥ e_k`, and in the data β(top1) correlates with the alignment `cos²θ₂` on the set inside *every* run (`+0.98, +0.58, +0.75, +0.93, +0.63`) and across runs (E0/E1 low `cos²θ₂ ≈ 0.3` ↔ low β; E3/E4/E2 high `cos²θ₂ ≈ 0.6–0.8` ↔ β ≈ 0.5–0.9). **`β_B(θ)` is the third-invariant / alignment coordinate of the hot cores (`ω ∥ e₃`-type stretching ⟹ `\det(∇u) > 0` ⟹ β ≪ 1; `ω ∥ e₂`-type ⟹ `\det(∇u) ≈ 0` ⟹ β ≈ 1), already represented among the preregistered observables by `cos²θ₂`; it does not determine C1 or C4, and they do not determine it, but the three are (datum-intensity, alignment) — two axes, not three.**
