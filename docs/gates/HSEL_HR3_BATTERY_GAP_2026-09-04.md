## §1 — HR-3′ counterexample-first battery

**The head, as the program reads it (P-1 §4 restatement + EQ-8 norm-uniform discipline):** for every certified solution and horizon `T′ ≤ T`, for every escape time `t` — a time with `Λ_τ > Λ_t` for all `τ ∈ (t, T_ref)` — there is `s ∈ [t + \tfrac{1}{4c(M)Λ_t}, t + \tfrac{1}{c(M)Λ_t}]` such that around **every** `x₀ ∈ ℝ³` the selected component set `V^{j,±}_s = \{ω_j^± > λΛ_s\}` is 1-D `δ`-sparse at some scale `ρ ≤ ρ_c(s)` in the weak sense (one direction suffices). Two readings of `T_ref`: **(H)** horizon reading `T_ref = T′` (the falsifiable norm-uniform form EQ-8 prescribes); **(H*)** blow-up reading `T_ref = T*` (BFG's own formulation, where `T*` is the first possible blow-up time).

**F-tube — the coherent-vortex-core family [D, formula level].** Let the maximal vorticity at time `s` sit in a coherent tube with Gaussian (Burgers-type) core `ω_j(r) = Λ_s e^{-r²/r_B²}` (distance `r` from the axis), circulation `Γ = πr_B²Λ_s`. Then

> **`r_B/(ν/Λ_s)^{1/2} = (Γ/(πν))^{1/2} = (Re_Γ/π)^{1/2}`** — the core radius exceeds the criterion's viscous scale by the square root of the circulation Reynolds number, *independently of `Λ_s`.*

The selected set at a core point is `\{ω_j > λΛ_s\} ⊇` the disk `r < r_B\sqrt{\ln(2M)}` (radius between `0.83r_B` and `1.05r_B`) times the tube's length. For `x₀` on the axis, every segment of every half-length `ρ ≤ ρ_c` in every direction lies inside the set whenever `ρ_c ≤ r_B\sqrt{\ln 2M}`, i.e. whenever

> **`Re_Γ ≥ π / (4\ln(2M)\,c(M)²)`** (`≤ 1.14/c(M)²`; for the local-existence constant `c(M) ≥ 1.07` this is `< 1`),

and then **no admissible `(ρ, d)` exists at `x₀`: the 1-D sparseness fails** (fraction `= 1 > δ`). Off-axis points at distance `a < r_B\sqrt{\ln 2M} − (2δ−1)ρ_c` from the axis fail likewise (the outward radial segment is the best direction; its inside-fraction is `1 − (a+ρ−r_B\sqrt{\ln 2M})/(2ρ) > δ`). The window `[t + 1/(4cΛ_t), t + 1/(cΛ_t)]` is shorter than the core's viscous time `r_B²/ν = Re_Γ/(πΛ_s) ≥ 1/(πΛ_s)` for `Re_Γ ≥ 1`... (more precisely the core persists over the window whenever `Re_Γ ≥ π/c(M)`), so no time in the window helps. The magnitude version (Grujić 2013 Thm 4.2, level `Λ_t/d₀^α ≤ λΛ_s`) has a larger set and fails a fortiori.

**Consequence under reading (H) — COUNTERFAMILY (with a construction debt).** Any certified solution possessing a *growth phase* — an interval on which `Λ` increases monotonically up to the horizon end, so that every time of the phase is an escape time relative to `T′` — whose maximum is carried by a coherent tube with `Re_Γ` above the threshold violates the head at every post-escape time of the phase, **per solution and irrespective of the uniformity of constants**. Such phases are the generic vortex-stretching transients of high-`Re_Γ` flows (a tube of core radius `K(ν/γ)^{1/2}`, `K > 2`, in the strain `γ` of neighbouring vortices contracts toward the Burgers radius with `Λ` increasing on the time scale `1/γ ≥ 1/Λ`, the ratio `r_B/(ν/Λ)^{1/2}` staying `(Re_Γ/π)^{1/2}` throughout since `Γ` is conserved to leading order). Existence of one such certified solution from Schwartz data is physically evident (vortex-ring collisions, antiparallel-tube interactions, turbulent worms with `Re_Γ ∼ Re_λ^{1/2} ≫ 1`) and is recorded as a **short-time construction debt** (a perturbative statement about smooth NS solutions — unlike the O-4 "sustained concentration" obstruction it needs no long-time control); the battery treats it as a counterfamily, not a kinematic stress, because the violating object is a *dynamical* phase of a smooth solution, not a snapshot.

## §2 — Gap decomposition: can `2/5 → 1/2` come out of NS?

**(D-1) Duality is dimension-capped [D].** BFG Lemma 24 is a lower bound `‖ω_j‖_{Ḣ^{-1}} ≥ c_*Λ r^{5/2}` for a super-level set that is not 3-D `δ`-sparse at scale `r` (test against a bump of height 1 on `B(x₀,r)`: `⟨ω_j, φ⟩ ≥ [λδ − (1−δ)]Λ|B_r| > 0` because `δ > 1/(1+λ)`, and `‖φ‖_{Ḣ¹} ∼ r^{1/2}`). Generally, a free bound `Φ(ω) ≤ B` with `Φ` positively homogeneous of degree 1 in `ω` and of length dimension `L^a` yields, by the same test, `Λ r^{2+a} ≲ B` on a non-sparse ball, i.e. sparseness at every `r ≥ (B/Λ)^{1/(2+a)}`:

> `‖ω‖_{Ḣ^{-1}} ≤ ‖u‖₂` (`a = 1/2`) ⟹ exponent `1/(2+1/2) = 2/5`; `‖ω‖_{L¹} ≤ C₀` (`a = 1`) ⟹ `1/3`; a time-window average of the enstrophy over the escape window (`a = 1`) ⟹ `1/3`; **the required `1/2` is `a = 0`: a dimensionless (scale-invariant) bound.** No free quantity of this program has `a < 1/2` (twenty-fourth record §2); interpolation between `Ḣ^{-1}` and `L¹` cannot lower `a` below `1/2`; the exponent `2/5` is therefore optimal for every duality argument fed by known a priori information. This is Albritton–Bradshaw's caution made quantitative: the duality route closes the gap **iff** a dimensionless a priori bound exists — the problem itself.

**(D-2) A dynamical closure cannot exist, because the target is false [D from §1].** Any argument (time selection in the escape window, analyticity, the maximum principle, the direction-twist dissipation, R1 of the twenty-fourth record, …) that derived "sparse at `ρ ≤ ρ_c` at some post-escape time" for *all* certified solutions would contradict F-tube. The analyticity/local-existence bound available in the window even works *against* sparseness near the maximum: `‖∇ω(s)‖_∞ ≤ C₁Λ_t^{3/2}` forces `\{ω_j^± > λΛ_s\} ⊇ B(x_{max}, (1−λ)Λ_s^{-1/2}/C₁)` — the set is *provably fat at the viscous scale* around the maximum, and the criterion's satisfiability at `x_{max}` is a constants condition `(1−λ)/C₁ ≤ δ/(2c(M))` on the local theory's constants, not a dynamical statement.

**(D-3) The only true form is the blow-up-restricted one, and it is a restatement [D].** Restricting the target to blow-up solutions near `T*` gives exactly the hypothesis of Thm 19 in its own setting; proving it is proving "blow-up ⟹ contradiction". Written as a single proposition, the residual content of HR-3′ is:

> *there is no certified solution with `T* < ∞` such that for all escape times `t` near `T*` and all `s` in the window some `x₀` has `V^{j(x₀),±}_s` occupying more than `δ` of every segment of every half-length `≤ ρ_c(s)` through `x₀`* — i.e. **exclusion of the "fat-core" blow-up class** (intense set 3-D-fat between the viscous scale `Λ^{-1/2}` and the energy scale `Λ^{-2/5}`; the model member is a shrinking coherent tube with `Re_Γ` fixed `≳ 1`, core `(Re_Γ/π)^{1/2}Λ^{-1/2} → 0`, not excluded by energy, by the a priori sparseness, or by any known theorem — Type-I self-similar profiles are excluded by Nečas–Růžička–Šverák/Tsai, but the fat-core class is not self-similar).

By Thm 19 this proposition is equivalent to "no fat-core blow-up", and since Thm 19 already excludes every *non*-fat blow-up, it is equivalent to global regularity of the certified class. **The gap `2/5 → 1/2` is therefore not a quantitative gap in an a priori estimate; it is the Clay problem restricted to the fat-core class, and the sparseness framework has already reduced the problem exactly to that class** — a genuine and useful reduction (due to Grujić–Bradshaw–Farhat, not to this program), but not a target that "a different use of existing theory" could prove without excluding fat-core blow-up directly.

**Answer to the commissioned question:** the `2/5 → 1/2` gap does not come out of NS by any a priori route (D-1: dimension cap; D-2: the target is false for regular flows), and its dynamical form is a restatement (D-3). What survives of the sparseness framework is the *reduction* "blow-up ⟹ fat-core blow-up", which is the correct place for any future work in this direction: a theorem excluding fat-core (tube/blob at the viscous scale, `Re_Γ ≳ 1`) singular limits. That is Clay-level in its own right and is **not** commissioned.
