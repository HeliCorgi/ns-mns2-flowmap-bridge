At a smooth off-axis positive nondegenerate maximum `x_*` of

\[
U=\frac{u^\theta}{r},\qquad
A=U(x_*),\qquad
q=\frac{S_*}{A},\qquad
S=-\frac{u^r}{r},

the previous record derived

\[
q'=A\,[\Pi-1-q^2+e+qd+m],
\]

where

\[
\Pi=\frac{(p_r/r)_*}{A^2},\qquad
 e=\frac{\nu(L_5S)_*}{A^2},\qquad
 d=-\frac{\nu(L_5U)_*}{A^2},\qquad
 m=\frac{V_*\cdot\nabla S_*}{A^2}.
\]

At the proposed lower cone boundary `q=1/4`, inward pointing is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
\Pi+e+\frac14d+m\ge\frac{17}{16}.
}
\tag{Q}
\]

The decision question is:

> **(Q-UNIV)** For every real, smooth, compactly supported, divergence-free,
> axisymmetric datum with swirl whose actual local Navier–Stokes solution has at
> `t=0` a unique positive nondegenerate off-axis maximum of `U` satisfying

\[
\boxed{\textbf{NO}.}

## 1. Pressure representation and the only nonlocal term in Q

For a smooth compactly supported divergence-free velocity field on `R^3`, taking the
divergence of Navier–Stokes gives

\[
-\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j).
\tag{1.1}
\]

With

\[
G(x)=\frac1{4\pi|x|},\qquad -\Delta G=\delta_0,
\]

the decaying pressure may be chosen as

\[
\boxed{
p(x)=\int_{\mathbb R^3}\partial_i\partial_jG(x-y)\,
        u_i(y)u_j(y)\,dy.
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

The additive pressure constant is irrelevant.  Since the construction below keeps a
neighborhood of `x_*` completely unchanged, the local quantities `A,q,d,e,m` are fixed.
The only Q term that can change is `Pi`, through the nonlocal pressure gradient.

## 2. Exact line-ring pressure sign

Fix cylindrical coordinates so that the target maximum is represented in Cartesian
coordinates by

\[
x_0=(r_0,0,0),\qquad 0<r_0<R.
\]

Consider the circle

\[
y_R(\theta)=R e_r(\theta)
=(R\cos\theta,R\sin\theta,0),
\]

with tangential unit vector

\[
e_\theta(\theta)=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta,0).
\]

The line-stress pressure generated by unit tangential stress along the ring is

\[
P_R(x)
:=R\int_0^{2\pi}
 e_\theta(\theta)^T D^2G(x-y_R(\theta))e_\theta(\theta)\,d\theta.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Define also the Newtonian ring potential

\[
J(r,R):=\int_0^{2\pi}G((r,0,0)-y_R(\theta))\,d\theta.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Differentiating in `theta` gives

\[
\partial_\theta^2G(x-y_R)
=R e_r\cdot\nabla_xG(x-y_R)
 +R^2 e_\theta^T D^2G(x-y_R)e_\theta.
\]

After integration over one period, the total `theta` derivative vanishes.  Since

\[
\partial_R G(x-y_R)=-e_r\cdot\nabla_xG(x-y_R),
\]

we obtain the exact identity

\[
\boxed{P_R(r,0,0)=\partial_RJ(r,R).}
\tag{2.3}
\]

For `0<r<R`, put `s=r/R`.  The absolutely convergent binomial/Fourier expansion

\[
(1-se^{i\theta})^{-1/2}
=\sum_{n=0}^\infty c_n s^n e^{in\theta},
\qquad
c_n=\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n}>0,
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
J(r,R)=\frac1{2R}
\sum_{n=0}^\infty c_n^2\left(\frac rR\right)^{2n}.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Termwise differentiation is justified on every compact subset of `0<=r<R`.  Hence

\[
\partial_r\partial_RJ(r,R)
=-\sum_{n=1}^\infty
 n(2n+1)c_n^2\,
 r^{2n-1}R^{-2n-2}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Every summand on the right is strictly negative.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\partial_rP_R(r_0,0,0)
=\partial_r\partial_RJ(r_0,R)<0
\qquad(0<r_0<R).
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

The leading far-ring term is

\[
\partial_rP_R(r_0,0,0)
=-\frac{3r_0}{4R^4}+O\!\left(\frac{r_0^3}{R^6}\right),
\]

but only the exact strict sign in (2.6) is needed.

## 3. Replace the line stress by a smooth compactly supported negative-swirl torus

Choose nonnegative

\[
\phi\in C_c^\infty((-1,1)^2),
\qquad
\int_{\mathbb R^2}\phi(\rho,\zeta)^2\,d\rho\,d\zeta=1.
\]

For sufficiently small `epsilon>0`, define an axisymmetric pure-swirl field supported in a
thin torus around `(r,z)=(R,0)` by

\[
w_{\epsilon,B}^\theta(r,z)
=-B\epsilon^{-1}
\phi\!\left(\frac{r-R}{\epsilon},\frac z\epsilon\right),
\qquad
w_{\epsilon,B}^r=w_{\epsilon,B}^z=0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Because the support stays a positive distance from the axis, this is a real
`C_c^infty(R^3)` axisymmetric vector field.  It is divergence-free: a theta-independent
pure-swirl field has zero three-dimensional divergence.  Moreover

\[
U_w=\frac{w_{\epsilon,B}^\theta}{r}\le0.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus this perturbation can never create a new **positive** maximum of `U`.

For a field point separated from the torus, the third derivatives of `G` are smooth on the
support.  In cylindrical volume measure,

\[
|w_{\epsilon,B}^\theta|^2\,r\,dr\,d\theta\,dz
\rightharpoonup
B^2R\,d\theta
\]

on the circle as `epsilon -> 0`.  Applying this weak convergence to the smooth pressure-
gradient kernel in (1.2) gives

\[
\partial_r p_{w_{\epsilon,B}}(x_0)
=B^2\bigl(C_{R,r_0}+o_{\epsilon\to0}(1)\bigr),
\tag{3.3}
\]

where

\[
C_{R,r_0}:=\partial_rP_R(x_0)<0
\]

by (2.6).  Fix one sufficiently small `epsilon` so that the coefficient
`C_epsilon<0`.  Then exactly by quadratic homogeneity,

\[
\boxed{
\partial_r p_{w_{\epsilon,B}}(x_0)=B^2 C_\epsilon
\longrightarrow-\infty
\quad\text{as }B\to\infty.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

No numerical sign inference is used here; the sign comes from the exact ring series.

## 4. A compactly supported core with the prescribed moving-max boundary jet

It remains to show that the pressure perturbation can be attached to a genuine admissible
axisymmetric core while keeping the Q-local data fixed.

Fix `x_0=(r_0,0)` in the `(r,z)` half-plane and choose a small compact neighborhood `K` of
`x_0` with positive distance from the axis and from the remote torus.

Choose a nonnegative bump `Phi in C_c^infty(K)` such that

- `Phi(x_0)=1`;
- `x_0` is its unique global maximum;
- `nabla Phi(x_0)=0`;
- `D^2 Phi(x_0)` is negative definite.

Set

\[
U_c=A\Phi.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Choose another cutoff `eta in C_c^infty(K)` equal to one on a neighborhood of `x_0`, and
set

\[
\psi_{1,c}(r,z)=\frac A4\,z\,\eta(r,z).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Recover the meridional velocity by the repository convention

\[
u_c^r=-r\partial_z\psi_{1,c},
\qquad
u_c^z=2\psi_{1,c}+r\partial_r\psi_{1,c},
\qquad
u_c^\theta=rU_c.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is a real, smooth, compactly supported, axisymmetric, divergence-free field with
nonzero swirl.  Near `x_0`,

\[
S_c=\partial_z\psi_{1,c}=\frac A4.
\]

Consequently at `x_0`,

\[
q=\frac14,
\qquad
\nabla S=0,
\qquad
L_5S=0,
\qquad
e=0,
\qquad m=0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Let

\[
\kappa:=-(L_5\Phi)(x_0)>0.
\]

Since `nabla Phi(x_0)=0`, the `3/r` part of `L_5` vanishes there and negativity of the
Hessian gives `kappa>0`.  Hence

\[
d=-\frac{\nu(L_5U_c)(x_0)}{A^2}
=\frac{\nu\kappa}{A}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Choose `A>8 nu kappa`.  Then

\[
0<d<\frac18.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus the core sits exactly on the proposed Q boundary and strictly below the D upper
boundary.

## 5. Remote-pressure poisoning theorem

Fix the core above and choose the thin torus support disjoint from `K`.  Define

\[
u_{0,B}:=u_c+w_{\epsilon,B}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Because the supports are disjoint,

\[
u_{0,B}\otimes u_{0,B}
=u_c\otimes u_c+w_{\epsilon,B}\otimes w_{\epsilon,B}
\tag{5.2}
\]

pointwise; there are no cross-stress terms.  Therefore the pressure gradients add exactly.
The remote torus vanishes on a whole neighborhood of `x_0`, so it changes none of

\[
A,q,d,e,m,H,\nabla S,L_5S,\nabla L_5U
\]

at `x_0`.  Because its normalized swirl is nonpositive, it also leaves `x_0` as the unique
positive global maximum of the total `U`.

Let `Pi_c` denote the normalized pressure contribution of the fixed core.  Then

\[
\Pi_B
=\Pi_c+\frac{B^2C_\epsilon}{r_0A^2},
\qquad C_\epsilon<0.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_B+e+\frac14d+m
\longrightarrow-\infty
\quad(B\to\infty).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

For all sufficiently large `B`,

\[
\Pi_B+e+\frac14d+m<\frac{17}{16},
\tag{5.5}
\]

so `(Q)` fails.

Every `u_{0,B}` is an admissible `C_c^infty` divergence-free datum for the current `R^3`,
`f=0`, axisymmetric-with-swirl track.  Standard local strong theory supplies the actual
Navier–Stokes trajectory from this datum.  At `t=0`, the exact moving-max identity from the
previous record therefore gives

\[
q'(0)
=A\left(\Pi_B+e+\frac14d+m-\frac{17}{16}\right)<0.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus the proposed lower Q boundary points **outward** for an actual local Navier–Stokes
solution.

### Decision theorem

> **Q-UNIV is false.**  There exist real `C_c^infty(R^3)` divergence-free
> axisymmetric-with-swirl data whose actual local Navier–Stokes solution has at `t=0` a
> unique positive nondegenerate off-axis maximum of `U`, with
> `q=1/4` and `0<d<1/8`, but with `q'(0)<0`; equivalently `(Q)` fails.
>
> Stronger lability statement: after fixing the complete local jet entering
> `A,q,d,e,m` at the maximum, the Q left-hand side can be made arbitrarily negative by a
> disjoint admissible negative-swirl torus.

## 6. What this kills, and what it does not

### Killed

1. **Universal Q barrier.**  `(Q)` is not a class-wide consequence of axisymmetry,
   incompressibility, compact support/Schwartz decay, or the Navier–Stokes pressure law.
2. **Local-jet Q proof.**  No argument depending only on the local maximum jet and on
   hypotheses preserved by the disjoint torus perturbation can prove `(Q)`: those inputs
   are identical while the conclusion changes sign.
3. **Automatic pressure rescue of the two-variable ODE skeleton.**  The nonlocal pressure
   can reinforce depletion rather than defeat it.

### Not killed

1. The algebraic implication
   `Q + D + cone entry -> A' >= (3/8) A^2` remains correct.
2. A **special datum** might still satisfy `(Q)` at every relevant first contact because
   of a genuinely global dynamical coherence condition.
3. The D-boundary condition is not decided here.
4. This record does not prove that every cone-entering trajectory exits, and does not prove
   or disprove finite-time Navier–Stokes breakdown.
5. It does not decide a positive-time first-contact theorem under extra global restrictions;
   the counterexample is already sufficient to refute the universal boundary statement at
   the initial contact.

Accordingly the old open label `Q-BARRIER` must be refined:

- **Q-UNIV:** `KILLED / NO` by this record;
- **Q-SPEC:** datum-specific global pressure/relay coherence along one candidate trajectory
  — **OPEN**.

Any future Q-SPEC proof must explicitly use a hypothesis that the remote-torus perturbation
violates.  Merely sharpening the local Hessian, curvature, maximum, or relay algebra cannot
repair Q-UNIV.
